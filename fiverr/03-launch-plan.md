# Fiverr launch plan

## Week 1 — set up

- [ ] Account, username (permanent — choose carefully), photo, tagline
- [ ] Profile description from `01-profile.md`
- [ ] Skills with honest levels
- [ ] **Publish gigs 1, 3 and 5 only** — landing page, Figma-to-code, bug fixing.
      Fiverr gives new gigs a temporary rotation boost in search, and firing all
      seven at once burns it across gigs you can't service at under 30 hrs/week.
- [ ] 3 images per gig (see below)
- [ ] Fill in FAQs on every gig — they're read more than sellers expect
- [ ] Set requirements: what you need from the buyer before you can start
- [ ] Connect Payoneer for withdrawal
- [ ] Install the mobile app and turn notifications on

## Gig images — the highest-return work here

Fiverr shows a **1280×769** thumbnail. Buyers decide from it.

- **Image 1** — a screenshot of real work with a short text overlay naming the
  outcome ("Admin dashboard with roles & permissions"). Not a stock photo, not
  a laptop on a desk, not your face.
- **Image 2** — a second real screen, or a before/after.
- **Image 3** — what's included, as a simple list on a clean background.

Rules: readable at thumbnail size, no more than about six words of overlay text,
consistent colours across all your gigs so they read as one seller. Blur any real
customer data.

A **video** raises conversion more than anything else you can do — 30–60 seconds,
your voice, showing a real project on screen. It's uncomfortable and it works.

## Week 2–4 — get the first order

Nobody buys from a zero-review seller by accident, so make the first orders easy
to say yes to:

- [ ] The **bug-fixing gig at $35** is the fastest route. It's an impulse
      purchase for someone whose site is down, and a 5-star review from a $35
      order counts exactly as much as one from a $500 order.
- [ ] Answer **Fiverr Briefs** (buyer requests) daily. Fresh sellers get seen
      there in a way they don't in search.
- [ ] Reply to every message within an hour. Response time is published and
      ranked.
- [ ] Deliver **before** the deadline you promised. Early delivery is the single
      most commented-on thing in reviews.
- [ ] Never ask for a review directly — it's against Fiverr's rules and can cost
      you the account. Deliver well and most buyers leave one.

## After the first 3 reviews

- [ ] Publish gigs 2 and 4 (admin dashboard, Supabase setup)
- [ ] Raise prices ~20% across the board
- [ ] Add the AI chatbot gig (6)
- [ ] Add gig extras everywhere — extras are where Fiverr margin actually lives

## After Level 1

- [ ] Publish gig 7 (web app → desktop)
- [ ] Raise prices again
- [ ] Consider a Fiverr Pro application once you have a solid review history

## Realistic expectations

- First order: **1–6 weeks**, mostly waiting rather than working. This is normal.
- Fiverr takes **20%**. A $200 order nets $160.
- Funds clear **14 days** after delivery (7 for higher levels). Don't plan around
  money you can't withdraw yet.
- Seller levels depend on order volume, ratings, on-time delivery and response
  rate over a rolling window. Fiverr changes the exact thresholds periodically —
  check the current requirements in your seller dashboard rather than trusting
  any number written down here.

## Don't

- Don't buy fake reviews. Detection is good and it's an instant permanent ban.
- Don't take payment or communication off-platform. Same outcome.
- Don't accept an order you can't deliver on time — a late delivery hurts your
  ranking far more than declining would have.
- Don't copy another seller's gig description. Fiverr checks, and buyers notice.
