# The team — presenting three developers honestly

Bilal Sagheer, Owais Khan (`github.com/owaisikhan`) and Ammar Sagheer
(`github.com/Ammar-Sagheer`) work together. Bilal is setting up the Upwork and
Fiverr profiles and finding work for all three.

## Why the team is actually a good pitch

These are not three of the same developer. They cover a pipeline:

| | Bilal | Owais | Ammar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core stack | Laravel, PHP, Vue, Node, Python | Next.js, React, Supabase | Next.js, React, Supabase |
| Depth | 8+ years, automation, AI/LLM, MCP, scraping | Postgres rules, RLS, Electron, offline licensing | Product side, design-to-code |
| Other | Associate Project Manager | Reviews and merges PRs | — |

A client who needs a Laravel API, a modern React front end, and it all delivered
on a schedule is one project for these three and three separate hires for
anyone else. **That** is the pitch — not "three web developers".

## The honest way to do this

Bilal's profile is built on **Bilal's own eight years of work**, which is a real
and strong record. He does not need to borrow anyone's projects, and he
shouldn't: a profile is a person, and clients test it on calls.

For the shared Next.js projects:

1. **Tick Upwork's "I worked on this with others" checkbox.** It exists for
   exactly this. An item flagged as collaborative with a clear role reads as
   professional. An unflagged one that a client later finds on someone else's
   GitHub reads as a lie and ends the contract.
2. **State his role in the first line** of the description. I've left `[FILL IN]`
   in `02-portfolio.md` rather than inventing one — put in what he actually did.
3. **Never claim to have personally written code he didn't write.** He can
   legitimately say "I led delivery on", "I coordinated", "I built the X part
   of" — whichever is true.

## The structure you actually want: an Upwork Agency

One individual profile in Bilal's name is what you've chosen for now, and it
works. But the structure that fits three people sharing work is an **Upwork
Agency**:

- Bilal creates the agency and is its owner; Owais and Ammar join as members.
- An agency can legitimately present **all** the team's work as agency work. The
  attribution problem disappears entirely.
- Bilal handles clients and delivery management; the other two build. That's
  exactly what an agency is for, and it's what he already does as an APM.
- You can bid on $3,000+ projects that no one of you would bid on alone.
- Each of them keeps an individual profile too, so they can still bid solo.

**Cost:** free to create. Same 10% fee.

**When:** after Bilal has 2–3 reviews on his individual profile. A brand-new
agency with no history sells worse than a new freelancer, because clients expect
an agency to have a track record. Get the reviews first, then form it and carry
them across.

## Rules that keep this from going wrong

1. **One person, one profile.** Never let two people answer messages as the same
   freelancer. Upwork detects it and it's an account-closure offence.
2. **Never bid on the same job from two profiles.** Clients notice immediately.
3. **Whoever is on the call must be able to answer for the work.** If a client
   wants to go deep on the Postgres trigger design in the pump system, that's
   Owais's call to be on. Plan for that rather than improvising.
4. **Get sign-off before publishing anything from private or client repos.**
   `Petrol-Pump-Management-Software`, `Offline-Petrol-Pump-Manager`,
   `saam-s-store` and `Coffee-Shop-Website` are private, and some have real
   clients behind them.
5. **Blur real data in screenshots** — customer names, balances, licence keys.
6. **Settle money in writing now.** How income splits, who owns the pump product
   (it's a licensed commercial product with real installs), what happens if one
   of you stops. Three-way partnerships between friends and family go wrong in
   exactly one predictable way, and it's entirely preventable.
