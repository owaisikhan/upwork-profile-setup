# 30-day launch plan

Ordered by what actually moves the needle. Do it top to bottom.

## Before you write a single proposal

- [ ] **Rename `merdian-consulting` → `meridian-consulting` on GitHub.** The
      typo is in your live URL and in the repo name. It's the first thing a
      client sees and it reads as careless. GitHub redirects the old URL, so
      nothing breaks. Redeploy on Vercel to get the corrected domain.
- [ ] **Write real READMEs** for `merdian-consulting`, `the-wild-oasis`,
      `Core-Stack`, `The-Ledger-Mobile`. Three of them currently ship the
      default `create-next-app` boilerplate. Your `pump-manager-display` and
      `windows-app-stocks-RSI` READMEs are genuinely excellent — match that.
      Clients do click through to GitHub.
- [ ] **Add descriptions and topics** to every repo you'd show a client. Empty
      description fields look abandoned.
- [ ] **Update your GitHub bio.** "Web Developer" is a wasted field. Use:
      `Next.js + Supabase. I build business software small companies run on.`
      Add your location and your portfolio link.
- [ ] **Archive or make private** the noise: `test`, `practice-react`,
      `helloWorld`, `coffee-shop-3` (empty), `Bootcamp-2020`. Keep the 2020
      assignments — they show history — but they shouldn't be what's on top.
- [ ] **Ship your portfolio site.** `portfolio-2-rho-umber.vercel.app` exists
      and has a real component library behind it. Point a proper domain at it
      (`owaiskhan.dev` or similar, ~$12/yr). A custom domain in your Upwork
      profile is worth more than it costs.
- [ ] **Take screenshots** of every live project. 1440px wide, real content.
      This is the single highest-return hour in this whole list.

## Week 1–2 — first proposals

Target **5 proposals a day**, tailored, on jobs where:
- Payment method is **verified**
- Client has **hired before** with decent review history
- Budget is **$150+** fixed or **$15+/hr**
- Posted in the **last 2 hours**
- Fewer than **10 proposals** so far

Deliberately bid slightly low on your first 2–3 jobs. You are buying reviews,
not earning. Once you have 3 five-star reviews the economics flip completely.

**First-job filter:** take small, boring, well-defined jobs. A $150 landing page
you deliver in 3 days with a 5-star review is worth more right now than a $1,200
project that drags 6 weeks and ends in a dispute.

## Week 3–4 — compound

- [ ] After 2 completed jobs: ask every client for a review the day you deliver
- [ ] After 3 five-star reviews: **raise to $25/hr**
- [ ] Refresh portfolio with the client work you just delivered
- [ ] Start turning down the bad-fit jobs

## The rate ladder

| Milestone | Rate |
|---|---|
| Now | $15/hr |
| 3 five-star reviews | $25/hr |
| 10 reviews / $1,000 earned | $35/hr |
| Top Rated badge | $45/hr+ |

Raise the rate as soon as you hit each mark. The mistake almost everyone makes
is staying at the starting rate for a year because it's working.

## Realistic expectations

- First job: **2–5 weeks** of consistent daily proposals. Not days.
- Expect ~40–60 proposals before the first win. That's normal, not failure.
- Job Success Score only appears after several completed contracts. Protect it —
  one dispute is expensive.
- Upwork takes 10%. Price with that in mind: a $500 project nets you $450.

## Skills gap worth closing (optional, 2–3 weeks)

Your stack is strong and current. Two additions would widen the job pool:
1. **Stripe integration.** A huge share of Upwork Next.js jobs mention payments,
   and you have no payment work in your repos. One weekend project fixes this.
2. **TypeScript as default.** You use it in `coffee-shop-2` and `the-wild-oasis`
   but most projects are plain JavaScript. Many higher-paying posts require TS
   explicitly. Convert one existing project and you can list it honestly.

Neither blocks you from starting today.
